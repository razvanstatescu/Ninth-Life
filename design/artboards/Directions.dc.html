Research and directions

AAC tools look like toys. Keypads look like banks. Night with one ember key was free.

What we looked at

Voice and feelings apps (Mobbin)

Kit, Tolan, Pi, Numo, Apple Health: cartoon companions, pastel tiles, emoji. Friendly, but childish for adults who type to talk. AssistiveWare's survey of 556 people found "talker" reads as babyish.

Number keypads (Mobbin)

Cash App, ANZ Plus, Alma, Shell, Alan: big digits, letters a few pixels tall or gone. We flip it: letters lead, the digit is the hint.

Accessibility pages (Mobbin)

Webflow, Miro, Figma, Microsoft AI: blue and bright primaries under giant grotesk headlines. Accessibility already wears blue.

Voice pickers (Mobbin)

ElevenLabs, Comet, Vocabulary, Medium: one row per voice with a play button and a check. We keep the pattern at 72px rows.

Type

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Mono from the Braille Institute: built for letterform distinction for low vision, free under the SIL Open Font License on Google Fonts.

Directions

Night keypad

Selected

Night ground, a moon bubble with cat ears, eight plum keys and one ember key. Reads at 16px and owns Halloween without a costume.

Phone shell

Dropped

A candybar phone outline. Reads instantly, but points at one maker's phone and invites trademark trouble.

Cat mascot

Dropped

A friendly cat character. Warm, but childish for adults, and a face competes with the person who is speaking.

Clinic blue

Dropped

White and calm blue. Trustworthy and invisible: most accessibility pages already look like this.
