# Outreach templates

Short messages asking for one 30-minute feedback session (or a forwarded call for volunteers). English and
Romanian. Last updated 2026-11-06. Protocol: [protocol.md](protocol.md). Consent: [consent.md](consent.md).

## Rules for every message

- **Write to organizations and public role addresses**, not to private individuals: a department's or association's
  contact address, a clinic's front desk, a lab's public contact. Placeholders below are roles, never names. Do not
  look up or message people with a condition directly, and never message someone because of their health.
- **One message and at most one follow-up after 7 days.** No reply means no. Stop at once if asked.
- **No medical claims.** Do not say the tool is "for" a condition, that it helps, treats or speeds anything up, or
  that it is tested or validated. Use the intended-purpose sentence as is.
- **No numbers** except from `packages/eval/RESULTS.md`, and only with "simulated typing test" next to them. Better: none.
- **Online communities** (for example subreddits for SLPs or AAC): read and follow each community's rules on
  self-promotion first; ask the moderators before posting; post feedback requests, not ads; never DM members
  who did not ask.
- Send from a team address. BCC never. Keep a private list of who was contacted and when (not in this repo).
- Replace `[APP_URL]`, `[REPO_URL]`, `[TEAM_EMAIL]`, `[YOUR FIRST NAME]` before sending.

Targets (from plan §7): SLP university departments in Bucharest and Cluj; AAC clinics and centres in Romania
and the UK; ALS/MND associations (as organizations that may forward a call to their professional staff or
members, never as "the users"); AAC text-entry research groups (for example the Michigan Tech group whose AAC
text datasets we use; write to the lab's public contact).

## 4. AAC research group (English)

**Subject:** Feedback request: an open-source 9-key typing-to-talk app with a proof check on LLM suggestions

Dear [Group lead / Lab contact],

We are a three-person team building Ninth Life for the Kiroween 2026 hackathon: an open-source web app where people
type one press per letter on keys 2 to 9 and the app speaks the sentence. An offline dictionary and bigram model decode
the keys; an optional language model only re-ranks words that already match the keys, and a deterministic validator
drops any word that does not, so nothing is spoken that the person did not type.

Your group's published AAC text resources informed our evaluation, and we credit them in our data notice
([REPO_URL]/blob/main/data/NOTICE.md). We would value 20 to 30 minutes of feedback from anyone in the lab on the method
or the interface, at any time before Nov 26. Our evaluation is a simulated-typing harness; method and results are in
[REPO_URL]/blob/main/packages/eval/RESULTS.md. We make no claims about real users.

CC BY 4.0 while the download's readme lists CC BY-ND 3.0. Which applies to deriving bigram counts from it? If ND applies,
we will rebuild our model without it.

Ninth Life is a general-purpose text entry and text-to-speech tool. It is not a medical device and is not intended to
